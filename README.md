# #IfThenSheCan: Three More Women in STEM
### Celebrating Chien-Shiung Wu, Maryam Mirzakhani, and Alice Augusta Ball Through 3D-Printed Public Art

## Project Overview

The **#IfThenSheCan** initiative celebrates women's achievements in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM), helping young girls see the possibilities of a future in STEM.

The original **#IfThenSheCan – The Exhibit** featured 120 life-size, 3D-printed statues of contemporary women STEM innovators.

This project proposes three additional women whose groundbreaking contributions to science deserve greater recognition:

1. **Chien-Shiung Wu** – Experimental Physics
2. **Maryam Mirzakhani** – Mathematics and Geometry
3. **Alice Augusta Ball** – Chemistry and Medical Research

These additions would recognize historical pioneers whose discoveries changed their respective fields and provide new opportunities to educate, inspire, and increase the visibility of women in STEM.

### Original 120 Roster Check

The official IF/THEN virtual exhibit directory was reviewed. None of these three names appear in its published directory of the Original 120:

- Chien-Shiung Wu
- Maryam Mirzakhani
- Alice Augusta Ball

This makes them proposed additions rather than repeat representations of women already included in that directory.

**Reference:** [Official IF/THEN Digital Exhibit – Original 120 Directory](https://ifthenexhibit.org/virtual_tour/)

# 1. Chien-Shiung Wu
### The First Lady of Physics

**1912–1997 | Experimental Nuclear Physics | Chinese-American**

## Background and Contributions

Chien-Shiung Wu was a pioneering experimental physicist whose research fundamentally changed the understanding of nuclear and particle physics.

Born in China, Wu moved to the United States to continue her education and eventually became a professor at Columbia University.

Her most significant contribution was the **Wu Experiment (1956)**, which experimentally demonstrated that parity conservation does not hold in weak nuclear interactions.

Parity conservation was a principle physicists had previously assumed applied universally to physical interactions.

Wu designed and conducted an experiment using cobalt-60 nuclei at extremely low temperatures. Her results confirmed the theoretical prediction made by physicists Tsung-Dao Lee and Chen-Ning Yang.

Lee and Yang received the 1957 Nobel Prize in Physics for their theoretical work. Wu was not included in the award, despite conducting the experiment that demonstrated the prediction.

Her contributions remain foundational to modern particle physics.

Other achievements included:
- Working on the Manhattan Project during World War II.
- Research involving beta decay and radioactive isotopes.
- Advancing experimental techniques in nuclear physics.
- Becoming a leading advocate for women in scientific research.

**Why she deserves recognition:**

Wu demonstrated that experimental science can challenge even the most widely accepted theories. Her achievements also highlight the importance of recognizing the contributions of women behind major scientific discoveries.

## Proposed CAD Statue Design

**Concept: The Woman Who Changed the Laws of Physics**

The statue would portray Chien-Shiung Wu standing confidently, holding a scientific notebook or a simplified physics experiment apparatus.

Suggested design elements:

- A laboratory notebook representing her experimental research.
- A small cobalt-60 experimental apparatus as a secondary sculpture element.
- Abstract particle paths or opposing arrows representing the parity experiment.
- A plaque explaining the Wu Experiment and its influence on particle physics.

### CAD Design Approach

The model could be divided into several components:

1. **Human figure:** Create a recognizable sculpted representation of Wu using historically appropriate photographic references.
2. **Clothing:** Model a professional mid-century outfit based on archival photographs.
3. **Scientific accessory:** Design a simplified experimental apparatus that communicates her research without attempting to reproduce complex laboratory equipment in full.
4. **Base:** Add an engraved title and a short explanation of the parity experiment.

The physical figure and scientific accessories could be separate CAD components, making them easier to print and assemble.

**Suggested inscription:**

"She challenged the laws of nature and changed our understanding of the universe."

# 2. Maryam Mirzakhani
### Breaking Barriers in Mathematics

**1977–2017 | Mathematics | Iranian**

## Background and Contributions

Maryam Mirzakhani was an Iranian mathematician renowned for her groundbreaking contributions to geometry and dynamical systems.

In 2014, she became the first woman and the first Iranian to receive the prestigious **Fields Medal**, one of the highest honours in mathematics.

Her research focused on complex mathematical structures, including:

- Hyperbolic geometry.
- Teichmüller theory.
- Dynamical systems.
- The geometry and symmetry of curved surfaces.
- Moduli spaces of Riemann surfaces.

Mirzakhani developed innovative approaches to understanding the geometry of surfaces and the behaviour of mathematical systems.

Her work helped expand knowledge in areas of mathematics that have connections to topology, geometry, and theoretical physics.

She also became an important role model for women pursuing advanced mathematics and scientific research.

**Why she deserves recognition:**

Mirzakhani broke a major barrier in the history of mathematics. Her career demonstrated that women can make extraordinary contributions to highly theoretical and advanced scientific disciplines.

## Proposed CAD Statue Design

**Concept: The Art and Geometry of Mathematics**

The statue would portray Maryam Mirzakhani holding a mathematical notebook, with a distinctive geometric sculpture incorporated into the design.

Suggested design elements:

- An open notebook representing her mathematical research.
- A three-dimensional curved surface inspired by hyperbolic geometry.
- Geometric patterns and interconnected surfaces.
- A small representation of the Fields Medal on the statue's base.

### CAD Design Approach

The model could include:

1. **Human figure:** A sculpted representation based on verified photographic references.
2. **Notebook:** A separate modeled component held in one hand.
3. **Mathematical sculpture:** A curved geometric form inspired by the surfaces studied in her research.
4. **Base:** Include her name, the year she received the Fields Medal, and a short explanation of her mathematical achievements.

The mathematical sculpture could be designed as a separate component to make printing easier and allow different geometric designs to be explored.

Rather than placing complicated equations randomly on the statue, the design should use mathematically meaningful surfaces and patterns.

**Suggested inscription:**

"She opened new dimensions in mathematics and inspired generations to think beyond boundaries."

# 3. Alice Augusta Ball
### A Revolutionary Chemist Whose Discovery Saved Lives

**1892–1916 | Chemistry and Medical Research | African-American**

## Background and Contributions

Alice Augusta Ball was an African-American chemist who developed an important treatment for Hansen's disease, historically known as leprosy.

She studied pharmaceutical chemistry and pharmacy at the University of Washington before completing her master's degree in chemistry at the College of Hawaiʻi in 1915.

At the time, chaulmoogra oil was used in attempts to treat Hansen's disease. However, existing preparations were difficult to administer and were not reliably absorbed by the body.

Ball developed a chemical method to isolate and modify the active fatty acids in chaulmoogra oil, producing ethyl esters that could be administered by injection.

This became known as the **Ball Method**.

Her work provided an important treatment that was used for decades before effective antibiotic treatments became available.

Tragically, Ball died in 1916 at only 24 years old, before she could see the full impact of her research.

Her supervisor, Arthur Dean, continued work on the treatment after her death. Her contribution was later more widely recognized.

Other important achievements:
- First Black woman to earn a master's degree in chemistry from the College of Hawaiʻi.
- First woman chemistry instructor at the College of Hawaiʻi.
- Developed an innovative chemical extraction and modification process.
- Helped advance the treatment of people affected by Hansen's disease.

**Why she deserves recognition:**

Alice Ball's story demonstrates how chemistry can improve human health. Her achievements are particularly remarkable given her age and the barriers faced by Black women in science during the early twentieth century.

## Proposed CAD Statue Design

**Concept: Chemistry That Changed Medicine**

The statue would portray Alice Ball standing in a laboratory environment, holding a chemistry flask or scientific notebook.

Suggested design elements:

- A laboratory flask representing her chemical research.
- A chaulmoogra branch or fruit to represent the original source of the oil.
- A simplified molecular model representing the chemical modification of fatty acids.
- A laboratory-inspired base featuring her name and scientific achievement.

### CAD Design Approach

The model could be created using the following components:

1. **Human figure:** A historically informed sculpted representation based on archival photographs.
2. **Laboratory flask:** A separate accessory designed to fit naturally into her hand.
3. **Botanical element:** A chaulmoogra branch with leaves and fruit, modeled as an additional component.
4. **Molecular structure:** A simplified scientific visualization of the chemistry behind the Ball Method.
5. **Base:** An engraved explanation of her contribution to medical chemistry.

The molecular model should be presented as an educational illustration rather than an exact representation of the complete chemical reaction.

**Suggested inscription:**

"Her discovery transformed medical treatment, and her legacy continues to inspire scientific innovation."

# CAD and 3D Printing Implementation

## Design Objective

The goal is to create three printable, recognizable statues that combine historical representation with visual elements explaining each woman's scientific contribution.

The models should be suitable for:
- STEM exhibitions.
- Educational displays.
- Public-art installations.
- Museums and science centres.
- Schools and university projects.

## Proposed CAD Workflow

### Step 1: Historical Research and Reference Collection

Before designing each statue, collect:

- Verified historical photographs.
- Biographical information.
- Scientific diagrams relevant to each woman's work.
- Reference images of clothing and laboratory equipment.
- Relevant objects associated with their discoveries.

The figures should be based on documented references rather than invented physical characteristics.

### Step 2: Create the Human Figure

Build a three-dimensional representation of each woman using a sculpting or CAD application.

Potential software:
- [Blender](https://www.blender.org/) – Digital sculpting and detailed character modeling.
- [Fusion](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/overview) – Mechanical components and accessory design.
- [FreeCAD](https://www.freecad.org/) – Open-source parametric CAD modeling.
- [ZBrush](https://www.maxon.net/en/zbrush) – Detailed digital sculpting.

Blender would be a strong starting point for creating the human figures and integrating their scientific accessories.

### Step 3: Add Scientific Accessories

Each statue should have a recognizable scientific element:

| Woman | Scientific accessory | Visual message |
|---|---|---|
| Chien-Shiung Wu | Simplified cobalt-60 experiment apparatus | Experimental physics |
| Maryam Mirzakhani | Curved geometric mathematical sculpture | Advanced mathematics |
| Alice Ball | Chemistry flask and chaulmoogra branch | Chemistry and medicine |

The accessories should be designed to complement the figure without overwhelming it.

### Step 4: Create the Statue Base

Each statue should include a stable base with:

- The woman's name.
- Her years of birth and death.
- Her STEM discipline.
- A short explanation of her contribution.
- An optional QR code linking to a digital biography.

The QR code could connect viewers to a website or project page containing additional educational material.

### Step 5: Prepare the Models for 3D Printing

Once the models are complete:

- Ensure the mesh is watertight and manifold.
- Check for intersecting or disconnected geometry.
- Give thin features appropriate thickness.
- Make sure the statue has a stable centre of gravity.
- Split complex accessories into separate printable components where necessary.
- Check overhangs and support requirements.
- Export printable files.

Recommended formats:

- `.STL` – Widely supported 3D-printing mesh format.
- `.3MF` – Useful for retaining additional manufacturing information.
- `.BLEND` – Editable Blender project file.
- `.STEP` – Useful for compatible CAD components and assemblies.

### Step 6: Print and Assemble

Possible production methods:

- FDM printing for larger statue prototypes and robust components.
- Resin printing for smaller, detailed accessories and facial features.
- Larger-format additive manufacturing for exhibition-scale statues.

The final material, print orientation, layer settings, and wall thickness should be selected according to the printer, size, intended environment, and available budget.

After printing, components can be assembled, sanded, finished, and painted.

A consistent colour scheme could connect all three statues while allowing their individual scientific accessories to stand out.

# Expected Project Outcomes

This project aims to:

1. Recognize three influential women whose STEM achievements are not represented in the published Original 120 directory.
2. Increase public awareness of women's historical contributions to physics, mathematics, and chemistry.
3. Translate scientific achievements into tangible, accessible works of public art.
4. Demonstrate how CAD and additive manufacturing can be used for STEM education.
5. Inspire younger generations, particularly girls, to explore scientific careers.
6. Create reusable digital models that can be shared for educational and exhibition purposes.

## Conclusion

Chien-Shiung Wu, Maryam Mirzakhani, and Alice Augusta Ball represent three extraordinary scientific journeys.

Their achievements challenged existing scientific understanding, advanced mathematical knowledge, and improved medical treatment.

Through CAD modeling and 3D printing, their stories can become more visible and accessible to the public.

**If she can see it, then she can be it.**
